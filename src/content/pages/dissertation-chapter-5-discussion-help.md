## What Chapter 5 Is For (and the One Rule That Matters Most)

Chapter 5 interprets findings already reported in [Chapter 4](/dissertation-chapter-4-results-help/) — it introduces zero new statistics. If a specific number, a *p*-value, an *F*-statistic, hasn't already appeared in Chapter 4, it does not belong here.

## The Standard Chapter 5 Structure

### The Seven Parts, in Order

1. Brief summary of the study's purpose and key findings
2. Findings discussed by research question
3. Comparison to prior literature
4. Theoretical and practical implications
5. Limitations
6. Recommendations for future research
7. Closing conclusion

## Comparing Your Findings to Prior Literature

For each major finding, state explicitly whether it **confirms**, **contradicts**, or **extends** a specific named prior study. Vague "this aligns with the literature" sentences without citing anything specific are one of the most common complaints committees raise about discussion chapters.

## Writing a Defensible Limitations Section

### Statistical Limitations Specifically

Go beyond generic limitations like "time constraints." Name statistical ones directly:

- Small sample size relative to your a priori power-analysis target
- Violated assumptions and their likely effect on your results
- Non-probability sampling limiting generalisability
- Cross-sectional design limiting any causal claims

These are more specific and more defensible than boilerplate limitations sections.

## Writing Future-Research Recommendations That Aren't Generic

Recommendations should flow directly from *this* study's specific limitations and findings — not a boilerplate "future research should use a larger sample" line that could apply to literally any study ever written.

## How to Discuss a Non-Significant Result

A non-significant finding is still a finding worth discussing. What does it suggest about the theory or the population? If your achieved sample fell short of your [a priori power-analysis target](/power-analysis-sample-size-assignment-help/), connect the null result back to that directly, rather than treating it as simply "no effect."
