## What Makes EdD Dissertation Statistics Different?

A Doctor of Education (EdD) dissertation is applied and practitioner-focused, distinct from a PhD in Education, which more often aims at generating generalisable theoretical knowledge. EdD research typically evaluates a specific program, intervention, or practice change within a real educational setting — a school, district, or institution — rather than testing broad theory across a wide population. That framing shapes which statistical methods actually fit the work.

## Common EdD Research Designs and Tests

EdD dissertations tend to cluster around a recognisable set of designs and tests:

- **Program-evaluation designs**, comparing outcomes before and after a new curriculum, intervention, or policy is introduced
- **Quasi-experimental classroom or school comparisons**, since true random assignment is rarely possible in real educational settings — see the full [one-way ANOVA guide](/one-way-anova-assignment-help/) for group-comparison designs, or [ANCOVA](/ancova-assignment-help/) when a covariate like prior achievement needs to be controlled for
- **Regression**, for identifying which factors (attendance, engagement, socioeconomic variables) predict a given educational outcome
- Occasionally repeated measures designs, tracking the same cohort's outcomes across multiple time points within a program

## Applied Research Framing vs Theoretical PhD Work

Because random assignment is often impractical in a school or district setting, quasi-experimental designs — comparing existing groups rather than randomly assigned ones — are the norm in EdD work, which makes assumption-checking and covariate control (ANCOVA in particular) more central than in a cleaner experimental design. If your program's expectations lean toward more theoretical, generalisable research questions requiring more advanced multivariate methods, see [PhD dissertation SPSS help](/phd-dissertation-spss-help/) instead — some education doctoral programs blur this line, so confirm with your committee which framing your specific dissertation is expected to follow.
