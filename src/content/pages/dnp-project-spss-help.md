## What Is a DNP Scholarly Project, Statistically?

A Doctor of Nursing Practice (DNP) scholarly project is fundamentally different from a PhD dissertation, even though both are doctoral-level work: a DNP project demonstrates a **practice improvement** (implementing and evaluating a change in clinical practice) rather than producing an original theoretical contribution to a field. That distinction shapes the statistics directly. DNP projects usually analyse **pre/post intervention data** or **quality-improvement metrics** from a specific practice site, not a large research sample built to test a general theory.

For subject-specific test guidance beyond this page, see [SPSS for nursing and healthcare research](/spss-for-nursing-healthcare-research/).

## Common DNP Statistical Methods

Because DNP projects are practice-focused rather than theory-focused, they tend to use a smaller, more applied set of tests:

- **Paired samples t-tests**, comparing an outcome before and after a practice change on the same unit or patient population. See the full [paired samples t-test guide](/paired-samples-t-test-assignment-help/)
- **Chi-square tests**, comparing categorical outcome rates (e.g. compliance rates, readmission rates) before and after an intervention
- **Descriptive statistics and quality-improvement metrics**, tracked over the project timeline rather than tested inferentially in every case
- Occasionally independent samples t-tests or one-way ANOVA, when comparing outcomes across different units or provider groups rather than the same group over time

Advanced multivariate methods (SEM, multilevel modelling) are uncommon in DNP work. The practice-improvement framing rarely calls for them, and a clean, correctly-run paired comparison is usually the stronger choice than an unnecessarily complex model.

## DNP vs PhD: Practice Change vs Original Research

A PhD dissertation in nursing or a related field is typically expected to generate new theoretical knowledge, often requiring more advanced statistical methods to support that claim. See [PhD dissertation SPSS help](/phd-dissertation-spss-help/) for that tier. A DNP project instead demonstrates that you can identify a practice problem, implement an evidence-based change, and evaluate its effect using appropriate, but not necessarily advanced, statistics. Choosing a simpler, well-justified test that matches your practice-improvement design is the right call here, not a limitation.

Not sure which test fits your project's outcome data? See the full [SPSS statistical test guide](/spss-statistical-tests-explained/), or [get help](/get-started/) with your DNP project statistics.
