Public health and epidemiological research leans on cohort and case-control studies, and health survey data, more than most other fields. If you're doing SPSS work for a public health assignment, MPH thesis, or epidemiology project, chances are it falls into one of these designs.

## The Research Designs That Shape Public Health Statistics

### Cohort Studies

Studies following a group over time to see who develops an outcome, typically analysed with survival analysis when time-to-event matters, or logistic regression when the outcome is simply present or absent by the end of follow-up.

### Case-Control Studies

Studies comparing people who already have an outcome (cases) against those who don't (controls), looking backward at exposure differences between the groups, most often analysed with logistic regression to estimate odds ratios.

### Health Survey Data

Cross-sectional studies measuring health behaviours, access, or outcomes in a population sample, typically analysed with chi-square for categorical associations or regression for predictor analysis.

## Odds Ratios, Relative Risk, and Getting the Right Measure

Epidemiological research reports risk differently depending on the study design. Case-control studies report **odds ratios** (from logistic regression), since true incidence can't be calculated when cases were deliberately oversampled. Cohort studies can report **relative risk** directly, since the full population at risk is followed over time. Confusing which measure your design actually supports, or reporting an odds ratio as if it were a relative risk, is a common and consequential error in public health write-ups.

## Frequently Asked Questions About SPSS for Public Health Research

**When do I report an odds ratio instead of relative risk?**
Report an odds ratio whenever your design is case-control (or when using logistic regression generally). Relative risk is only directly calculable in cohort or experimental designs where you know the true incidence in each group.

**What SPSS module do I need for survival analysis?**
Base SPSS Statistics includes the Survival module for Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox regression; most university licenses include it, but confirm with your department.

**My exposure variable has more than two categories: does that change the test?**
Chi-square and logistic regression both handle multi-category exposures, though logistic regression requires choosing a reference category and interpreting each odds ratio relative to it.
