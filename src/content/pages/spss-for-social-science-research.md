Social science research (sociology, political science, and public policy) leans on large-scale survey data and secondary analysis of public datasets more than most other fields. If you're doing SPSS work for a social science assignment, thesis, or policy research project, chances are it involves one of these approaches.

## The Research Designs That Shape Social Science Statistics

### Large-Scale Survey Data

Studies analysing primary survey data collected from a sample of the public, often examining relationships between demographic, attitudinal, and behavioural variables.

### Secondary Analysis of Public Datasets

Studies analysing existing public datasets (census data, national surveys, government statistics), which raises specific considerations around sample weighting and the categorical structure of pre-coded variables.

### Policy and Behaviour Outcome Studies

Studies examining what predicts a specific behaviour or policy-relevant outcome, such as voting behaviour, program uptake, or compliance with a policy.

## The Tests Social Science Assignments Use Most

- **Crosstabs and chi-square**, for examining associations between categorical variables (e.g. demographic group and voting preference). See [Chi-Square Test Assignment Help](/chi-square-test-assignment-help/)
- **Multiple regression**, for identifying which factors predict a continuous social or policy outcome. See [Multiple Linear Regression Assignment Help](/multiple-linear-regression-assignment-help/)
- **Logistic regression**, for predicting a binary policy or behaviour outcome (voted or didn't, adopted a policy or didn't). See [Binary Logistic Regression Assignment Help](/binary-logistic-regression-assignment-help/)

## Sample Weighting: The Step Secondary Data Analysis Can't Skip

When you're working with a public dataset built from a complex survey design (stratified or clustered sampling), running your analysis on the raw, unweighted data can produce a sample that doesn't represent the population it claims to. Most large public datasets include a weight variable specifically for this reason; check your dataset's documentation for which weight variable applies and how SPSS's Complex Samples module (or a simple weight command) should be applied before running your analysis.

## Before You Run Anything: Does Your Study Need Ethics Approval?

Social science research involving human participants, even anonymous survey data, routinely requires IRB or ethics board approval before data collection begins. Secondary analysis of already-public, de-identified datasets often has lighter requirements, but confirm this with your department rather than assuming. ## Frequently Asked Questions About SPSS for Social Science Research

**Do I need to weight my data if I'm using a public dataset?**
Check the dataset's documentation. Most major public surveys and census products include a sampling weight, and ignoring it can bias your results toward whichever groups were oversampled.

**What's the difference between a chi-square test and logistic regression for a binary outcome?**
Chi-square tests whether two categorical variables are associated at all; logistic regression predicts the binary outcome from one or more predictors simultaneously and gives you an odds ratio for each one's independent effect.

**My research spans multiple countries or regions: does that change the analysis?**
Cross-national or multi-level data (respondents nested within countries or regions) may call for multilevel modelling rather than standard regression. See the full [multilevel modelling guide](/multilevel-hierarchical-linear-modelling-assignment-help/) if that applies to your design.
